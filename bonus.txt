Lab 6 - Extra Credit

QUESTION
Which counties outside Illinois contribute the largest net migration gains
to Cook County?

DISCOVERED ANSWER
Jefferson County, Kentucky, contributes the largest estimated net gain to
Cook County: 433 people. Its estimated inflow to Cook County is 637 people,
compared with an outflow of 204 (637 - 204 = 433).

The 10 selected counties, ranked by net gain to Cook County, are:

Rank  County and state                   Inflow   Outflow   Net gain
 1    Jefferson County, Kentucky            637       204        433
 2    St. Joseph County, Indiana            792       433        359
 3    Baltimore City, Maryland              534       188        346
 4    Prince George's County, Maryland      411        81        330
 5    Hartford County, Connecticut          335        38        297
 6    Travis County, Texas                  880       629        251
 7    New Haven County, Connecticut         420       190        230
 8    Canyon County, Idaho                  225         0        225
 9    El Paso County, Texas                 308        90        218
10    Stanislaus County, California         207         0        207

These counties span seven states. Together they account for an estimated
inflow of 4,749 people and outflow of 1,853, giving Cook County a net gain
of 2,896 people across the 10 selected county pairs.

The largest net gain is different from the largest inflow: Travis County
has the largest inflow among these selected counties (880), but its 629
people moving in the opposite direction reduce the net gain to 251.

TIE AT TENTH PLACE
York County, South Carolina, also has a net gain of 207 (237 inflow minus
30 outflow). To keep exactly 10 flows, equal net gains are ordered by county
GEOID, smallest first. Stanislaus County (06099) is selected ahead of York
County (45091); the equal estimates do not establish a meaningful difference
between their net gains.

METHOD
1. Use the same Cook County migration data as the required map, including
   the migration values restored from the original Census table.
2. Keep counties where State_B is not Illinois and Net_B2A is greater than zero.
3. Sort Net_B2A from largest to smallest, breaking ties by county GEOID.
4. Select the first 10 counties.
5. Map from County B into Cook County (County A).

Net gain = Net_B2A = Flow_B2A - Flow_A2B (inflow minus outflow).
The figures are ACS 2016-2020 survey estimates. The combined net gain above
applies only to the selected county pairs, not Cook County's overall migration
balance or total population change. These are estimates, not exact counts.

MAP
Open bonus.html. The title is a Leaflet control pane. Hover over each origin
circle to see its county, state, and estimated net gain to Cook County. The
Cook County circle is labeled as the destination. The green lines animate
into Cook County; their widths and shades show the size of the net gains.

Legend classes match the map:
  400-499 people: 8px, #005A32
  300-399 people: 6px, #238B45
  200-299 people: 3.5px, #41AB5D
